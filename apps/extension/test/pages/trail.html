Field notes: tide pools

Tide pools form where the sea leaves water behind in hollows of rock at low tide, and the creatures in them live through heat, salt and waves twice a day.

Ochre sea stars hold on with hundreds of tube feet and pry mussels open slowly, over hours, by tiring the muscle that keeps the shell shut.

Anemones close into soft green knobs when the water leaves, keeping a little sea inside them until the tide comes back.
